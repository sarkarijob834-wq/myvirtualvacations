What Is A Virtual Vacation?

Quick answer: a virtual vacation is a trip you take online. Instead of boarding a plane, you open a website and explore real places through walking footage, driving views, aerial flights, live cameras and map-based videos.

The idea is simple: travel with your eyes and ears. The official Virtual Vacation website calls itself a site that lets you experience the world from home. It offers walking and driving tours for 100+ cities, flying tours for 80+ cities and window views from hundreds of cities.

This guide explains every tour type, how to plan a session, who benefits most and how to get a relaxing, useful experience. Features of third-party sites can change, so confirm details on the official pages.

Virtual Walking Tours: Stroll 100+ Cities

A virtual walking tour is the closest thing to wandering a new city. You move at street level, notice shopfronts, hear traffic and chatter, and choose your own turns.

Try it when you want ambience more than facts. Pick a city that is awake in the evening, put on headphones and walk without a goal for 15 minutes. Open the Walking Tour.

Driving Tours And Virtual Road Trips

The driving tour puts you behind the wheel of a camera car in 100+ cities. The road trip mode lets you drive between cities instead of staying in one.

Great for long, quiet sessions. Pair a driving tour with music and imagine the journey. Start with the Driving Tour or the Road Trip.

Flying Tours: Cities From Above

Aerial views reveal how a city fits together: rivers, bridges, harbors and skylines. The official site lists flying tours for 80+ cities.

Use a flying tour to understand a destination's layout before choosing where to stay. See the Flying Tour.

Live Cam Travel: See The World Right Now

Live cams show what is happening in a city at this moment: weather, crowds, light and sunsets. That real-time feel makes a virtual vacation more personal.

Tip: choose a city in a different time zone to catch sunrise at lunchtime. Open the Live Cam section and keep in mind that feeds can go offline.

Virtual Window Views

Monuments And VidEarth

The Monuments section covers famous and lesser-known sites. VidEarth lets you click cities on a map to watch videos from them, which makes it easy to follow curiosity.

Try one monument a day and read its history afterward. Visit Monuments and VidEarth.

City Guesser: Play A Travel Game

The City Guesser drops you into a city without telling you where. Look at signs, architecture, vehicles and plants, then guess. It suits families, classrooms and friends.

The official site says it was inspired by GeoGuessr. Play the City Guesser with a notebook for clues.

Best Setup For An Immersive Experience

Use the biggest screen you have, or cast to a TV. Headphones matter because street sound sells the illusion. Close other tabs, switch on full screen and keep a stable connection.

  • Large screen or TV
  • Headphones or speakers
  • Fast, stable internet
  • Notifications off
  • Snacks from the country you are visiting

Relax With Virtual Travel

Looking at calm scenery can lower the feeling of stress for many people. Use a slow window view, keep sessions short and breathe steadily. This is relaxation, not medical care. If you struggle with anxiety, talk to a professional.

Virtual Vacations For Learning

Teachers can combine a walking tour with a geography worksheet. Ask students to list ten things they see, then compare cities. The City Guesser turns observation into a game. Preview any tour before class.

Safety And Privacy

Open only the official link, avoid look-alike pages, never enter payment details on a free tour site and keep your browser updated. Review the official mission and privacy page for current policies.

Background: Where Virtual Travel Came From

Armchair travel is older than the internet: travel books, panoramas, slide shows and television travel programmes all let people visit places in imagination. Digital maps and street-level imagery, such as Google Earth in the mid-2000s and Street View from 2007, made exploring real streets from a screen routine.

The Virtual Vacation website built on this idea with walking, driving and flying tours. Public descriptions say it uses crowdsourced first-person videos, and that its City Guesser game was inspired by GeoGuessr. We could not verify when the site launched, and a homeschool podcast episode reviewed it on April 9, 2025. Treat dates and numbers cautiously, since third-party sites change.

Tips For A Better Virtual Vacation

  • Pick one city and one tour type per session.
  • Use headphones; street sound is half the experience.
  • Pause often and look for details such as signs, plants and vehicles.
  • Keep notes for real trip planning, then verify current conditions.
  • Take breaks so your eyes and attention stay fresh.

Troubleshooting

  • Video will not play: refresh, update your browser and try another network.
  • Stuttering: close tabs, lower video quality if offered and restart your device.
  • No sound: check the tab is not muted and volume is up.
  • Live cam offline: feeds can go down, so try another city.

Virtual Vacation FAQ

What is a virtual vacation?

A virtual vacation is a trip you take on a screen. You explore cities through walking, driving or flying footage, live cams and map-based videos without leaving home.

Is a virtual vacation free?

The official Virtual Vacation website lets you start tours, live cams and games in your browser. Always check the site for current terms.

Do I need to download an app?

No. The tours run in a modern browser on desktop, tablet or phone.

How many cities can I visit?

The official site lists walking and driving tours across 100+ cities, flying tours for 80+ cities and window views from hundreds of cities.

What is a virtual walking tour?

It is a first-person walk through a city with real street scenery and ambient sound, so you feel like you are strolling its streets.

What is the City Guesser game?

A guessing game that drops you into a city. You look around, listen and try to work out where you are.

Are live cams really live?

The Live Cam section shows live footage from cities. Availability depends on the camera feeds, so some may be offline at times.

Can a virtual vacation replace real travel?

It cannot replace the full experience, but it is a good way to relax, plan, learn or travel when budget, health or time limits you.

Is it good for kids and classrooms?

Yes. Teachers and parents use virtual tours to teach geography and culture. Preview content first and guide younger children.

Can I use it for trip planning?

Yes. Walking a street or viewing a monument first helps you decide what to see in person.

Does it work on a phone?

Modern mobile browsers can run the tours. A larger screen or casting to a TV gives a more immersive view.

What is the best device for a virtual vacation?

A big monitor or TV with good speakers or headphones. A stable internet connection matters most.

What is VidEarth?

A tool where you click cities on a world map to watch videos from them.

How do I relax during a virtual vacation?

Dim the lights, use headphones, pick a calm city or window view and give yourself 20 to 30 minutes without notifications.

Is this website the official Virtual Vacation?

No. My Virtual Vacations is an independent guide and is not affiliated with virtualvacation.us. Use the official link to start your tours.

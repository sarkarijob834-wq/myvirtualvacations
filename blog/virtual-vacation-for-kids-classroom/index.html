Virtual Vacation for Kids and Classrooms: Lesson Ideas

Virtual travel is a handy geography tool at home or school. These classroom-friendly activities use walking tours, flying views and guessing games.

Why it works for learning

Seeing real streets, buildings and landscapes makes geography concrete. A homeschool podcast episode also suggests the Virtual Vacation site for geography and cultural studies.

Preview first

Always watch a tour yourself before sharing it with children, since crowdsourced footage can include anything ordinary streets contain.

Observation worksheet

Ask students to list ten things they see, then compare two cities by transport, signs and buildings.

Guessing game lessons

A city guessing game teaches clue-spotting: language on signs, driving side, climate and architecture. Debrief by asking which clues were most useful.

Cross-curricular ideas

Write a short story set in a city, practise listening to a language, or chart weather across time zones.

Keep it short and safe

Limit sessions to twenty minutes, supervise younger children and follow school internet rules.
